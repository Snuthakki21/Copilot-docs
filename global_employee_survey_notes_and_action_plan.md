# Global Employee Survey Discussion Notes

## Meeting Purpose

The team’s survey results were reported as the lowest within the broader organization. The purpose of the discussion was to:

- Understand the reasons behind the scores.
- Collect context that was not available through survey comments.
- Identify recurring themes.
- Develop an action plan to improve the employee experience and future survey results.

## Participation and Survey Context

- Seven of 11 employees responded, resulting in a 64% participation rate.
- With only seven responses, each response represents approximately 14 percentage points.
- The manager could not view the written survey comments because the number of comments or commenters did not meet the anonymity threshold.
- The team was asked to provide candid verbal feedback to explain the results.
- The manager emphasized the importance of full participation in future surveys.

# Key Discussion Notes

## 1. Workload and Capacity

- The team has lost employees through resignations and workforce reductions.
- Vacant positions have not always been backfilled.
- Remaining employees are expected to manage:
  - Daily application support.
  - Production support.
  - Project delivery.
  - Vulnerabilities.
  - TCI events.
  - Migrations.
  - Automated testing.
  - Compliance-related work.
  - AI-related goals.
- Deadlines are often aggressive or change after work has already been prioritized.
- Employees frequently have to stop current work to address a new urgent request.
- The cumulative workload contributes to stress, anxiety, and difficulty feeling a sense of accomplishment.
- Employees were encouraged to raise capacity concerns so management can help prioritize or reassign work.

## 2. Job Security and Organizational Trust

- Job security was one of the strongest concerns raised.
- Employees described ongoing anxiety about layoffs and displacement.
- Public statements about further workforce reductions have increased uncertainty.
- Some employees expressed apprehension around the Tuesday following payday because of prior displacement patterns.
- The push to develop AI tools is viewed by some employees as a possible threat to their own roles.
- Rumors about reducing staffing through performance management are contributing to fear, even though those rumors were not confirmed.
- Employees clarified that these concerns are primarily related to messaging and decisions from senior leadership, not the direct management team.

## 3. Tools, Processes, Documentation, and Training

- Vulnerability tools may identify issues inconsistently across multiple cycles.
- Dashboards and documentation are not always current or consistent.
- Confluence pages and process instructions frequently change.
- Office hours do not always provide definitive answers.
- Employees may be redirected between several teams before receiving guidance.
- Some training materials consist mainly of recorded office hours rather than structured instruction.
- Employees often feel they must independently determine processes while also meeting urgent deadlines.
- The team requested:
  - Stable, step-by-step documentation.
  - Clear process ownership.
  - Role-specific training.
  - Better support for newer tools, platforms, migrations, and vulnerabilities.

## 4. Communication and Transparency

- Employees sometimes learn about major company developments from external news sources before receiving internal communication.
- The team would like more timely explanations of:
  - Workforce decisions.
  - Organizational changes.
  - Business strategy.
  - Expected employee impact.
- Limited communication contributes to rumors and uncertainty.
- Some employees are skeptical that the survey is fully anonymous, which may affect participation and honesty.

## 5. Pride in and Willingness to Recommend the Company

- Past sales-practice issues continue to affect public perception and employee pride.
- Current workforce reductions, job insecurity, and workload pressure make employees hesitant to recommend Wells Fargo as a place to work.
- Employees also identified positive aspects of employment:
  - Competitive salary.
  - Benefits.
  - Hybrid-work flexibility.
  - Flexibility for appointments and personal responsibilities.
  - Strong relationships within the immediate team.
- These positive factors may explain why some employees selected neutral instead of unfavorable responses.

## 6. Customer Focus

- Employees generally believe they remain committed and responsive to customers.
- A large amount of current work is focused on:
  - Compliance.
  - Vulnerability remediation.
  - Migrations.
  - Operational requirements.
- These responsibilities can reduce the time available for customer-facing enhancements.
- Employees often have to choose between mandatory operational work and work that more directly benefits customers.
- The term “customer” was interpreted differently:
  - External banking customers.
  - Internal business partners.
  - Application users.
  - Partner teams.
- This ambiguity may have affected survey responses.

## 7. Team Collaboration and Cross-Functional Collaboration

- The immediate team was described as supportive, responsive, and willing to help.
- Employees said they are comfortable asking teammates for assistance.
- Lower cooperation scores may reflect interactions with external teams rather than the immediate team.
- Examples of cross-functional challenges included:
  - Delays from platform, infrastructure, database, or CSA teams.
  - Conflicting priorities across teams.
  - Different delivery timelines.
  - Inconsistent guidance.
  - Required dependencies that do not share the same urgency.
- The phrase “people I work with” may have been interpreted as either the immediate team or all partner teams.

## 8. Personal Accomplishment and Recognition

- Employees find it difficult to feel accomplished when:
  - New work is continually added.
  - Goals expand each year.
  - Completed work is immediately replaced by another urgent task.
  - Daily responsibilities are treated only as baseline expectations.
  - Additional initiatives are required to receive a “meets expectations” rating.
- Employees would like more recognition for:
  - Keeping applications operational.
  - Production support.
  - Project delivery.
  - Compliance work.
  - Migration work.
  - Absorbing new responsibilities.

## 9. Meaning of “Helping Wells Fargo Succeed”

- The corrected score showed that 71% of respondents were willing to put in significant effort.
- Employees emphasized that they continue to work sincerely and ethically.
- Employees remain committed to customers and to doing quality work.
- The term “succeed” was interpreted differently:
  - Customer outcomes.
  - Company stability.
  - Shareholder returns.
  - Financial performance.
- Employees may feel stronger commitment to customer success than to financial or shareholder outcomes.

## 10. Survey Design and Interpretation Concerns

- Several questions were considered too broad or ambiguous.
- Different employees may have interpreted key terms differently.
- The small number of respondents magnified the impact of individual answers.
- The lack of written comments limited the manager’s ability to understand the scores.
- The team felt that the results should not automatically be interpreted as evidence that the immediate team is disengaged or dysfunctional.
- A pre-survey discussion was proposed to clarify terminology and question intent before the next survey.

# Overall Takeaway

The discussion showed a disconnect between the survey scores and employees’ view of the immediate team.

Employees generally described the direct team as:

- Collaborative.
- Supportive.
- Hardworking.
- Customer-focused.
- Highly responsive.

The lower scores appear to be driven primarily by:

- Enterprise-level workforce uncertainty.
- Heavy workload and insufficient capacity.
- Aggressive and changing priorities.
- Unstable processes and tools.
- Inadequate training and documentation.
- Cross-functional obstacles.
- Limited communication from senior leadership.
- Ambiguous survey wording.

The team remains committed to doing quality work, but engagement and trust are being strained by the broader work environment.

# Action Plan Categories

## 1. Capacity and Prioritization

### Objective

Reduce overload and create a more transparent and sustainable approach to prioritizing work.

### Potential Actions

- Create a consolidated view of all active work, including operational, project, compliance, vulnerability, migration, and AI initiatives.
- Establish clear priority levels and escalation criteria.
- Review workload and capacity regularly during team meetings.
- Identify work that can be delayed, reassigned, simplified, or stopped.
- Document resource gaps and escalate them to leadership.
- Clarify which work takes priority when multiple urgent requests conflict.
- Encourage employees to raise capacity concerns early without fear of negative consequences.

### Measures of Progress

- Fewer conflicting urgent requests.
- Reduced after-hours or unplanned work.
- Improved clarity on priorities.
- Better employee feedback on workload manageability.

## 2. Processes, Tools, and Enablement

### Objective

Reduce time lost to unclear processes, inconsistent tools, and incomplete guidance.

### Potential Actions

- Create stable, step-by-step process documentation.
- Assign an owner to each critical process.
- Maintain a single source of truth for current instructions.
- Add version dates and change logs to process documentation.
- Replace informal recordings with structured training materials where possible.
- Develop role-specific training for vulnerabilities, migrations, installations, and new tools.
- Track recurring process issues and escalate them to the appropriate owning teams.
- Create an internal troubleshooting guide for common issues.

### Measures of Progress

- Reduced time spent searching for answers.
- Fewer repeated questions and escalations.
- Improved training-related survey scores.
- Fewer process-related delays.

## 3. Communication and Transparency

### Objective

Improve trust by providing timely, clear, and consistent information.

### Potential Actions

- Share relevant organizational updates with the team promptly.
- Explain what is known, what is not known, and what decisions are pending.
- Communicate the company’s perspective on major external news when appropriate.
- Create a regular forum for questions about organizational changes.
- Address rumors directly when factual information is available.
- Escalate recurring employee concerns to senior leadership.
- Reinforce how survey anonymity and confidentiality are handled.

### Measures of Progress

- Reduced reliance on rumors.
- Improved trust-related survey scores.
- Increased employee participation in discussions.
- Fewer reports of learning company information first through external sources.

## 4. Recognition and Employee Experience

### Objective

Increase employees’ sense of accomplishment, appreciation, and connection to their work.

### Potential Actions

- Recognize operational, production-support, compliance, and maintenance work.
- Highlight completed work and business impact during team meetings.
- Ensure goals reflect actual capacity and the full scope of responsibilities.
- Review whether “meets expectations” criteria reasonably account for daily operational work.
- Celebrate milestone completion before moving immediately to the next priority.
- Provide more frequent, specific recognition from managers and partners.
- Connect technical work to customer and business outcomes.

### Measures of Progress

- Improved sense-of-accomplishment scores.
- Increased employee recognition.
- Better alignment between goals and actual work.
- Positive feedback about management support.

## 5. Cross-Functional Collaboration

### Objective

Improve cooperation, accountability, and delivery across dependent teams.

### Potential Actions

- Identify the external teams that most frequently create delays or conflicting guidance.
- Define clear owners, response expectations, and escalation paths.
- Create shared delivery plans for work with multiple dependencies.
- Track unresolved cross-functional blockers.
- Escalate repeated delays with documented examples.
- Clarify responsibilities between application, platform, infrastructure, database, and support teams.
- Establish joint working sessions for complex or recurring issues.

### Measures of Progress

- Faster resolution of dependencies.
- Fewer conflicting instructions.
- Reduced delays caused by external teams.
- Improved cooperation and information-sharing scores.

## 6. Customer Focus and Work Alignment

### Objective

Protect time for customer-value work while meeting mandatory operational requirements.

### Potential Actions

- Separate customer-facing work from compliance, remediation, and migration work in planning.
- Make tradeoffs visible when mandatory work delays customer enhancements.
- Review whether current priorities align with customer and business value.
- Define who the “customer” is for each team or application.
- Include customer-impact statements in major work items.
- Track time spent on customer enhancements versus mandatory operational work.

### Measures of Progress

- Improved customer-responsiveness scores.
- More visible customer-value delivery.
- Clearer prioritization decisions.
- Better understanding of customer impact.

## 7. Survey Preparation and Participation

### Objective

Improve the accuracy, consistency, and usefulness of future survey results.

### Potential Actions

- Meet before the next survey to review key terms and question intent.
- Clarify terms such as:
  - Customer.
  - Department or team.
  - People I work with.
  - Training.
  - Company success.
- Encourage all employees to participate.
- Reinforce that responses should be honest and based on a shared understanding of the question.
- Remind employees to provide comments where possible.
- Validate all reported survey figures before presenting them.
- Compare future scores against both prior-year results and appropriate peer groups.

### Measures of Progress

- Higher participation rate.
- Fewer ambiguous responses.
- More written comments.
- Greater confidence in the accuracy of the results.
